HAPT is a vocabulary for describing who deploys high-risk AI systems, where and for what purpose, who is affected, and which oversight, instructions, governance and complaint mechanisms apply. Its taxonomies follow the elements of the fundamental rights impact assessment in Article 27 of the EU AI Act.

This is an unofficial draft. The machine-readable files hapt.ttl, hapt.rdf and hapt.jsonld are the normative source; this page is generated from them.

Introduction

HAPT organises the concepts needed to describe the deployment of a high-risk AI system under the EU AI Act. It grew from an entity mapping of high-risk AI actors and extends it with taxonomies for each element that a deployer must describe in a fundamental rights impact assessment (FRIA).

Every concept is a skos:Concept in one concept scheme. Concepts are organised with skos:broader and skos:narrower, linked across taxonomies with skos:related, and each taxonomy is a skos:Collection. HAPT reuses existing vocabularies instead of redefining them: concepts are mapped to DPV and its EU AI Act extension, AIRO and VAIR with skos:exactMatch, skos:closeMatch and skos:broadMatch.

Reading the concept tables

Parent lists the concept's skos:broader concepts; a concept can have more than one. At the root of a taxonomy, Parent shows the external class the taxonomy narrows (skos:broadMatch). Related lists skos:related links, which are symmetric and never connect concepts in the same hierarchical chain. Also in names other taxonomies whose collection includes the concept. Open questions

Modelling decisions to review, collected from the editorial notes.

  • Human Subject: DPV places Human Subject under Legal Entity; the source diagram places it under Natural Person, which is kept here.
  • AI Office: DPV's EU AI Act extension classifies the AI Office as an Authority; the source diagram places it under Legal Entity.
  • EU Database Management: The narrower items are IT systems rather than entities. Consider a separate taxonomy of EU large-scale IT systems (AI Act Annex X).
  • Eurodac: Spelled 'Eurodact' in the source diagram; kept as a hidden label so searches still match.
  • Critical Digital Infrastructure Management Authorities: The source diagram repeats the parent's name for both children; they were renamed Private/Public to match the other utilities.
  • Critical Digital Infrastructure Operation Authorities: The source diagram repeats the parent's name for both children; they were renamed Private/Public to match the other utilities.
  • Gas Management Authorities: Has no incoming arrow from Deployers in the source diagram; placed here to match its siblings.
  • Education Sector Deployer: Placeholder in the source diagram; the narrower concepts were added from VAIR.
  • Employment Sector Deployer: Placeholder in the source diagram; the narrower concepts were added.
  • Legal Aid Organisations: Placed under Prosecutorial Bodies in the source diagram, although legal aid usually serves the defence.
  • Law Enforcement Sector Deployer: Not broken down in the source diagram; the narrower concept was added from VAIR.
  • National Competent Authority: Resolves the open question in the source diagram ('Organisation or national competent authority?') by following DPV's EU AI Act extension: National Competent Authority sits under Authority, and Market Surveillance Authority under National Competent Authority.
  • Electoral Commission: Grouped under 'AuthorisedRepresentative' in the source diagram's Democratic Process branch. In the AI Act, an authorised representative acts for a non-EU provider, so this concept is placed under Authority.
  • Media Regulators: Grouped under 'AuthorisedRepresentative' in the source diagram; see Electoral Commission.
  • Sector: Annex III also covers biometrics (point 1), which is not yet modelled.
