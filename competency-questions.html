This page sets out the requirements of HAPT as an Ontology Requirements Specification Document (ORSD) and the competency questions HAPT must answer to describe a high-risk use case: a processing activity that is, or is not, required to have a DPIA under the GDPR, or an AI system that is high-risk under Annex III of the AI Act. Three use cases taken from the HAPT condition files illustrate the questions.

This is a draft. The competency questions have not yet been evaluated with SPARQL queries or SHACL shapes.

Ontology Requirements Specification

PurposeThe purpose of HAPT is to provide a controlled vocabulary of the terms needed to describe processing activities and AI systems precisely enough to determine whether they meet a condition under which an impact assessment is, or is not, required.
ScopeThe processing activities listed in the Data Protection Authority blacklists (DPIA Required lists, GDPR Art. 35(4)) and whitelists (DPIA Not Required lists, Art. 35(5)), the criteria of GDPR Art. 35(3) and the EDPB-endorsed DPIA guidelines, and the high-risk AI systems in AI Act Annex III.
Implementation languageW3C RDFS and SKOS.
Intended end-usersOrganisations who create and use DPIAs and FRIAs, and maintainers of legal compliance vocabularies such as DPV.
Intended uses Use 1: to describe use cases of data processing and AI systems.
Use 2: to identify the conditions in law and guidance under which a use case requires, or does not require, an impact assessment.
Non-functional requirements NFR1 Interoperability: the vocabulary should extend existing legal compliance ontologies such as DPV, AIRO and VAIR.
NFR2 Scalability: the vocabulary should be adaptable and extensible.
NFR3 Usability: the vocabulary should be usable by legal and non-legal stakeholders.
Functional requirementsThe competency questions in , grouped as HCQG1 (describing a use case) and HCQG2 (exceptions and the sources of a DPIA requirement).

Competency Questions

There is one competency question for each column of the Annex III analysis, which is the information needed to compare a use case with the DPIA and Annex III conditions. Where AIRO already asks the same question for identifying high-risk AI systems (Golpayegani et al., 2022, Table 1), the HAPT question follows its wording and the AIRO concepts are listed as equivalents. HCQ14 is proposed in addition to the columns so that DPIA Not Required conditions are covered.

Overlap with the IMPACTO competency questions

HAPT describes the use case; IMPACTO describes the impact assessment of it. The questions meet where the description decides whether, and by whom, an assessment must be carried out.

HAPTIMPACTOShared topic
HCQ11CQ1Both ask why a DPIA is required. IMPACTO records the outcome on the DPIA necessity assessment; HAPT records which GDPR provision or condition makes the use case DPIA required.
HCQ13CQ4Same question about jurisdictions. HAPT answers it for a use case through its links to the Member State conditions; IMPACTO answers it for an organisation's own process.
HCQ14CQ3Both describe a process that does not require a DPIA under Art. 35(5).
HCQ9CQ2, CQ8The organisation in HAPT is the controller in a DPIA (IMPACTO CQ2) and the deployer in a FRIA (IMPACTO CQ8).
HCQ10CQ9Exceptions: HAPT records the exceptions written into Annex III and the DPIA lists; IMPACTO records the Art. 27(1) exemption from the FRIA.
HCQ2, HCQ5, HCQ8CQ11Purpose, technology and the persons affected are the content of a FRIA under Art. 27(1)(a) and (c), so a HAPT description can be reused in an IMPACTO FRIA.
